\begin{abstract}
DeepFake generation techniques using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and diffusion models can now produce highly realistic synthetic faces. Although modern convolutional networks like EfficientNet-B3 achieve high accuracy on uncompressed benchmark datasets, their performance degrades noticeably when images are shared across social media platforms. Transmission pipelines introduce lossy JPEG compression that weakens fine forensic artifacts while introducing Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) block noise. To address this problem, this study proposes an attention-enhanced framework termed Coord-EfficientNet-B3. The method integrates Coordinate Attention into an EfficientNet-B3 backbone and employs stochastic compression-aware training. By factorizing spatial pooling into orthogonal horizontal and vertical coordinate representations, the network captures positional tampering traces while filtering out two-dimensional compression noise. Across 12,000 evaluations spanning multiple compression levels, the proposed framework reduces total compressed errors by 14.4\% and decreases false alarms by 24.2\% compared to the standard EfficientNet-B3 baseline ($p = 0.0037$, McNemar test). On an unseen holdout of face portraits, the model cuts false negatives by 50.0\% at the standard 0.50 threshold. Grad-CAM visualizations further confirm that Coordinate Attention avoids compression grid artifacts and focuses on facial manipulation boundaries.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The rapid growth of generative artificial intelligence has made it easy to produce realistic synthetic and manipulated images. Modern Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) such as StyleGAN, along with recent diffusion models, can create human facial portraits with natural skin textures, convincing lighting, and accurate facial expressions \cite{karras2019style}. While these tools have useful applications in creative media, they also present serious risks when used for impersonation, political deception, and online fraud \cite{mirsky2021creation}. Reliable detection of manipulated media has therefore become an essential research topic in digital forensics.

Deep learning architectures based on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are widely used for deepfake detection because they automatically extract hierarchical visual patterns from image pixels \cite{tan2019efficientnet}. EfficientNet architectures, in particular, provide a practical balance between model parameter size and representational capacity through compound scaling. Recently, Deepa et al. \cite{deepa2026enhanced} evaluated an EfficientNet-B3 baseline on deepfake image classification, demonstrating that it reaches over 97.9\% accuracy on clean benchmark datasets and outperforms traditional machine learning approaches like Random Forests and Support Vector Machines.

However, an important practical challenge remains unaddressed in standard evaluations. When images are transmitted across social media networks and messaging applications, they are routinely resized and compressed using lossy formats such as JPEG \cite{verdoliva2020media}. This process applies Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) quantization, which attenuates high-frequency details where subtle forensic traces usually appear \cite{rossler2019faceforensics}. At the same time, block boundaries in JPEG compression can produce artificial grid ringing that resembles tampering seams \cite{bondi2020training}. Consequently, conventional classifiers trained only on clean imagery often experience elevated false alarm rates and missed detections when evaluated on compressed media.

To tackle this limitation, this study incorporates Coordinate Attention \cite{hou2021coordinate} into the EfficientNet-B3 backbone alongside compression-aware stochastic training. While standard channel attention methods such as Squeeze-and-Excitation compress all spatial information into a single scalar value \cite{hu2018squeeze}, Coordinate Attention splits spatial pooling into separate horizontal and vertical 1D feature vectors. This allows the network to locate forgery cues along one spatial coordinate while preserving a wide receptive field along the other. In addition, Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) \cite{selvaraju2017gradcam} is applied to inspect the spatial focus of the model under compression.

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We design a Coordinate-Attention enhanced EfficientNet-B3 architecture (Coord-EfficientNet-B3) that captures precise positional manipulation cues without substantial parameter overhead.
    \item We implement a compression-aware training strategy that randomly applies varying JPEG quality factors during training, encouraging feature representations to remain robust under lossy compression.
    \item We conduct a structured benchmark across 12,000 evaluations over six compression levels, showing that the proposed model reduces total compressed errors by 14.4\% and cuts false alarms by 24.2\% ($p < 0.01$, McNemar test) compared to the un-augmented EfficientNet-B3 baseline \cite{deepa2026enhanced}.
    \item We evaluate an independent unseen holdout of pure facial portraits, demonstrating a 49.1\% reduction in total errors and a 50.0\% reduction in false negatives at the standard threshold ($\tau = 0.50$).
    \item We provide Grad-CAM visual analyses verifying that Coordinate Attention prevents feature dispersion across 2D compression grids and maintains focus on facial boundary regions.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{DeepFake Detection and CNN Models}
Early forensic techniques focused on hand-crafted biological and physical cues, including irregular eye blinking patterns \cite{li2018ictu}, abnormal corneal specular reflections, and head pose mismatches \cite{mirsky2021creation}. As generative models improved, these visible flaws became less frequent, prompting the adoption of deep feature extractors \cite{karras2019style}.

Convolutional networks have since become the standard tool for image manipulation analysis. Afchar et al. \cite{afchar2018mesonet} developed MesoNet, using compact convolutional layers to identify facial boundary artifacts. R{\"o}ssler et al. \cite{rossler2019faceforensics} conducted comprehensive benchmarks using XceptionNet on the FaceForensics++ dataset, demonstrating strong detection accuracy on uncompressed video frames. Tan and Le \cite{tan2019efficientnet} introduced EfficientNet, which scales network depth, width, and resolution systematically. Deepa et al. \cite{deepa2026enhanced} applied EfficientNet-B3 to deepfake detection, obtaining 97.95\% accuracy on clean benchmark imagery and showing that deep feature learning surpasses traditional texture descriptors. However, their study evaluated models solely on uncompressed data.

\subsection{Compression Robustness in Image Forensics}
Lossy compression represents a known challenge in real-world forensic deployment. JPEG encoding partitions an image into $8 \times 8$ pixel blocks, applies DCT transformation, and discards high-frequency coefficients through quantization tables \cite{verdoliva2020media}. R{\"o}ssler et al. \cite{rossler2019faceforensics} observed that deep models trained on pristine images suffer marked accuracy drops when tested on compressed images. Bondi et al. \cite{bondi2020training} showed that incorporating compression augmentations during training improves general stability. Nevertheless, standard convolutional filters can still struggle to distinguish authentic image details from block boundary artifacts when compression is severe.

\subsection{Attention Mechanisms in Computer Vision}
Attention modules help convolutional networks emphasize informative feature regions while down-weighting irrelevant background noise. Hu et al. \cite{hu2018squeeze} developed Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) networks, computing channel-wise attention weights through global average pooling. Woo et al. \cite{woo2018cbam} introduced the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM), combining channel and spatial attention sequentially.

Although helpful, standard attention designs have limitations in forensic tasks. SE pooling removes spatial coordinate information completely \cite{hou2021coordinate}. Standard 2D spatial attention captures broad areas but lacks directional selectivity, which can cause activations to align with regular 2D JPEG block grids. Hou et al. \cite{hou2021coordinate} addressed this by introducing Coordinate Attention, which factorizes 2D pooling into orthogonal 1D horizontal and vertical feature vectors. This design captures position-sensitive cues with low computational cost, making it suitable for identifying subtle tampering boundaries under compression.

\subsection{Visual Explainability via Grad-CAM}
To understand deep model decisions, visual attribution methods have gained widespread use. Selvaraju et al. \cite{selvaraju2017gradcam} proposed Grad-CAM, which calculates gradients of the predicted class score with respect to the final convolutional feature maps to produce localization heatmaps. Chattopadhay et al. \cite{chattopadhay2018gradcamplus} introduced Grad-CAM++ for fine-grained multi-region visualization. In this study, Grad-CAM is utilized to verify whether models attend to genuine facial forgery cues rather than compression noise.

\section{Proposed Methodology}

The overall pipeline of the proposed \textbf{Coord-EfficientNet-B3} framework is illustrated in Fig. \ref{fig:arch}. It consists of: (1) stochastic compression augmentation during data loading, (2) deep feature extraction via an EfficientNet-B3 backbone, (3) Coordinate Attention encoding, (4) a multilayer perceptron classifier head, and (5) Grad-CAM visual attribution. Sample images from the dataset are shown in Fig. \ref{fig:samples}.

\subsection{Backbone Feature Extraction}
The model takes an input face image $X \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 224 \times 224}$ and passes it through an ImageNet-pretrained EfficientNet-B3 backbone \cite{tan2019efficientnet}. This produces an intermediate feature map:
\begin{equation}
F = \Phi_{\text{backbone}}(X) \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H \times W}
\end{equation}
where $C = 1536$ represents the channel depth and $H = W = 7$ denotes the spatial resolution.

\subsection{Coordinate Attention Module}
To help the network capture localized manipulation traces and resist isotropic compression noise, Coordinate Attention \cite{hou2021coordinate} is placed after the final convolutional stage.

Standard global average pooling flattens all spatial values into a single number per channel, losing position details. Coordinate Attention instead pools features along horizontal and vertical directions separately. For channel $c$, pooling across width $W$ gives horizontal vector $z_c^h \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times 1}$, and pooling across height $H$ gives vertical vector $z_c^w \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times W}$:
\begin{equation}
z_c^h(h) = \frac{1}{W} \sum_{j=0}^{W-1} F_c(h, j), \quad z_c^w(w) = \frac{1}{H} \sum_{i=0}^{H-1} F_c(i, w)
\end{equation}

The vectors $z^h$ and $z^w$ are concatenated and passed through a shared $1 \times 1$ convolution, Batch Normalization, and a non-linear activation with a reduction ratio $r = 16$. The intermediate representation is then split and passed through two separate $1 \times 1$ convolutions and sigmoid functions, yielding horizontal weights $g^h \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H \times 1}$ and vertical weights $g^w \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times 1 \times W}$. The final modulated feature map $Y \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H \times W}$ is computed by element-wise multiplication:
\begin{equation}
Y_c(i, j) = F_c(i, j) \times g_c^h(i) \times g_c^w(j)
\end{equation}
This formulation allows the network to emphasize important spatial coordinates while preserving channel-wise selectivity.

\subsection{Classifier Head and Decision Rule}
The modulated feature tensor $Y$ is reduced through Global Average Pooling into a 1536-dimensional vector $\bar{y}$. This vector is passed to a three-stage classifier head:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Dense layer: $1536 \to 128$, with ReLU and Dropout (rate = 0.3)
    \item Dense layer: $128 \to 64$, with ReLU and Dropout (rate = 0.2)
    \item Output layer: $64 \to 1$, producing unnormalized scalar logit $z$.
\end{itemize}
The estimated probability of an image being a deepfake is obtained through the sigmoid activation:
\begin{equation}
P(\text{Fake} \mid X) = \sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}
\end{equation}
Predictions are assigned using the standard decision threshold $\tau = 0.50$, where an image is classified as Fake if $P(\text{Fake} \mid X) \ge 0.50$ and Real otherwise.

\subsection{Compression-Aware Training}
During training, input images are stochastically compressed with 50\% probability using random JPEG quality factors:
\begin{equation}
\text{QF} \sim \text{Uniform}(\{20, 40, 50, 60, 80\})
\end{equation}
This trains the network to rely on features that remain recognizable under different compression tiers. Models are trained using Binary Cross-Entropy loss:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = - [y \log \sigma(z) + (1 - y) \log (1 - \sigma(z))]
\end{equation}
using the Adam optimizer with learning rate $\eta = 10^{-4}$ and batch size $B = 16$ across 10 epochs. The first two epochs train only the classification head with a frozen backbone, while epochs 3 to 10 fine-tune all parameters end-to-end.

\subsection{Grad-CAM Interpretability}
To examine model predictions, Grad-CAM \cite{selvaraju2017gradcam} is applied. Gradients of the pre-activation score $y^c = z$ (for Fake) or $y^c = -z$ (for Real) are computed with respect to the Coordinate Attention feature map $Y$. Feature maps are weighted by their average gradients and combined using a ReLU activation to generate a spatial heatmap indicating the regions responsible for the decision.

\section{Experimental Setup and Results}

\subsection{Dataset Partitioning}
Experiments were carried out using the benchmark dataset curated by Karki \cite{karki2021deepfake}, which contains 140,000 balanced $256 \times 256$ facial images. Authentic images are sourced from Flickr-Faces-HQ (FFHQ), and synthetic faces are generated using NVIDIA StyleGAN \cite{karras2019style}.

A duplicate-checked partition was constructed:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Training Set:} 7,200 images (3,600 Real, 3,600 Fake)
    \item \textbf{Validation Set:} 800 clean images (400 Real, 400 Fake) for model checkpoint selection
    \item \textbf{Test Set:} 2,000 images (1,000 Real, 1,000 Fake) evaluated across multiple compression levels
\end{itemize}
SHA-256 and difference-hashing (dHash) audits confirmed that no identical images crossed between training, validation, and test splits.

\subsection{Grad-CAM Qualitative Findings}
Visualizations from Grad-CAM show that when compression is heavy (QF20), baseline EfficientNet-B3 heatmaps often scatter along repetitive square boundaries corresponding to $8 \times 8$ JPEG blocks. In contrast, Coordinate Attention uses directional pooling that filters out grid artifacts and concentrates attention on authentic facial boundaries, such as the eyes, nose, and jawline contours.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

\subsection{Mechanistic Basis of Compression Resistance}
JPEG compression works by quantizing $8 \times 8$ pixel frequency blocks, creating small step changes along grid edges. Standard 2D convolutions easily register these discontinuities as edges. In contrast, Coordinate Attention aggregates features using 1D projections along horizontal and vertical lines. This directional averaging smooths out local block boundaries while preserving continuous anatomical contours that extend across larger spatial dimensions.

\subsection{Limitations}
This study has two main limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The current setup evaluates static $256 \times 256$ face crops. Real-world video manipulations also contain temporal inconsistencies across frames that require sequential modeling.
    \item Experiments used simulated JPEG compression with fixed quality factors. Social media platforms often combine resizing, format conversion, and custom compression algorithms.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
This study presented Coord-EfficientNet-B3, an attention-enhanced deep learning model designed for deepfake detection under lossy social media compression. Across 12,000 evaluations on compressed images, the proposed architecture reduced overall errors by 14.4\% and decreased false alarms by 24.2\% compared to the standard EfficientNet-B3 baseline. On an unseen facial holdout, it halved missed detections (-50.0\% false negatives). Grad-CAM visualizations verified that Coordinate Attention prevents feature dispersion across compression grids. Future work will investigate extending this mechanism to video forensics and emerging diffusion-based synthetic media.
