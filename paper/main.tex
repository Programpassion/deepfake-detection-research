\begin{abstract}
Recent advances in generative networks allow the creation of photorealistic synthetic faces that threaten digital trust and information integrity. Standard convolutional architectures such as EfficientNet-B3 deliver strong accuracy on clean benchmarks, yet suffer significant performance drops under lossy social media compression. Transmission pipelines introduce Discrete Cosine Transform quantization noise that erases subtle forensic artifacts while generating deceptive block boundary edges. To address this issue, we introduce Coord-EfficientNet-B3, an attention enhanced framework combining Coordinate Attention with stochastic compression aware training. By factorizing spatial pooling into orthogonal horizontal and vertical coordinate representations, the model isolates structural tampering cues while ignoring two dimensional compression artifacts. Evaluated across 12,000 multi-quality factor inferences, the proposed architecture reduces overall compressed errors by 14.4\% and suppresses false alarms by 24.2\% relative to the unaugmented baseline ($p = 0.00116$, McNemar test). On an unseen holdout of face portraits, the model lowers total errors by 49.1\% and halves false negatives at the standard threshold.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The rapid rise of generative artificial intelligence has made it straightforward to produce realistic synthetic and manipulated imagery. Modern Generative Adversarial Networks such as StyleGAN, along with contemporary neural diffusion models, can synthesize human facial portraits featuring natural skin textures, convincing lighting, and authentic expressions \cite{goodfellow2014generative, karras2019style}. Although these synthesis technologies offer promising opportunities in digital entertainment and creative arts, they present serious risks when weaponized for impersonation, identity fraud, and political disinformation \cite{mirsky2021creation}. Establishing robust forensic methods capable of verifying digital authenticity in real world dissemination pipelines is therefore vital.

Deep learning architectures based on Convolutional Neural Networks have emerged as the standard foundation for deepfake detection because they extract hierarchical visual representations directly from pixel inputs \cite{tan2019efficientnet}. EfficientNet architectures offer a balanced combination of computational efficiency and representational capacity through compound scaling. Deepa et al. \cite{deepa2026enhanced} examined an EfficientNet B3 baseline for facial manipulation detection, demonstrating that it surpasses classical machine learning algorithms such as Random Forests and Support Vector Machines by reaching over 97.9\% accuracy on clean benchmark imagery.

Nevertheless, an unresolved operational vulnerability arises when forensic models encounter everyday social media transmissions. Messaging services and social networks routinely apply lossy JPEG compression to reduce storage requirements and bandwidth usage \cite{verdoliva2020media}. This process applies Discrete Cosine Transform quantization tables that discard subtle high frequency forensic artifacts where generative traces usually reside \cite{rossler2019faceforensics}. Simultaneously, block boundaries from lossy compression introduce artificial grid ringing that standard convolutional kernels often mistake for genuine manipulation boundaries \cite{bondi2020training}. Consequently, conventional classifiers trained strictly on pristine images suffer marked increases in false alarms and missed manipulations when tested on compressed content.

To resolve this limitation, we introduce Coord EfficientNet B3, integrating Coordinate Attention \cite{hou2021coordinate} into the EfficientNet B3 backbone alongside stochastic compression aware training. Unlike conventional channel attention modules that condense all spatial details into single scalar values \cite{hu2018squeeze}, Coordinate Attention factorizes spatial pooling into separate horizontal and vertical one dimensional feature vectors. This structural separation allows the network to capture location sensitive forensic cues along one axis while preserving a broad spatial context along the other, preventing feature dispersion across compression block grids.

The primary contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We design Coord EfficientNet B3, an attention enhanced architecture that integrates Coordinate Attention into the final stage of EfficientNet B3 to preserve directional manipulation traces with minimal parameter overhead.
    \item We implement a stochastic compression aware training routine that exposes the backbone to randomized lossy JPEG quality factors, encouraging the network to learn compression invariant forensic representations.
    \item We execute an extensive evaluation across 12,000 inferences spanning six compression quality tiers, establishing that the proposed framework cuts total compressed errors by 14.4\% and reduces false positives by 24.2\% ($p = 0.00116$, McNemar test) compared to the standard EfficientNet B3 baseline \cite{deepa2026enhanced}.
    \item We benchmark an independent holdout of 200 unseen facial portraits across all six compression tiers, demonstrating a 49.1\% reduction in total misclassifications and a 50.0\% reduction in false negatives at the standard decision threshold.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{DeepFake Detection and CNN Backbones}
Early forensic methodologies relied primarily on handcrafted physical and biological anomalies, such as irregular blink frequencies \cite{li2018ictu}, corneal reflection discrepancies, and head pose irregularities \cite{mirsky2021creation}. However, as generative architectures matured \cite{karras2019style}, these explicit physiological imperfections became increasingly imperceptible, compelling researchers to adopt deep representation learning.

Afchar et al. \cite{afchar2018mesonet} developed MesoNet, utilizing compact convolutional layers to identify mesoscopic facial tampering boundaries. Wang et al. \cite{wang2020cnn} demonstrated that CNN-generated images share common frequency domain artifacts that convolutional models can reliably recognize, though these signatures remain sensitive to post-processing. Chai et al. \cite{chai2020what} examined localized patch artifacts, revealing that synthetic imagery retains structural flaws across specific facial components. Tan and Le \cite{tan2019efficientnet} proposed EfficientNet, which scales depth, width, and resolution uniformly. Building on this backbone, Deepa et al. \cite{deepa2026enhanced} applied EfficientNet-B3 to deepfake detection, attaining 97.95\% accuracy on uncompressed benchmark datasets and confirming that deep representations substantially outperform traditional hand-crafted texture classifiers.

\subsection{Robustness Under Social Media Compression}
The vulnerability of deep neural classifiers to image compression is widely acknowledged in multimedia forensics. JPEG compression divides an image into $8 \times 8$ pixel grids, applies the Discrete Cosine Transform, and quantizes frequency coefficients \cite{verdoliva2020media}. R{\"o}ssler et al. \cite{rossler2019faceforensics} demonstrated on the FaceForensics++ benchmark that models trained exclusively on pristine imagery experience steep performance degradations when evaluated on compressed videos. Bondi et al. \cite{bondi2020training} confirmed that incorporating data augmentation during training enhances classification stability. However, standard convolutional filters remain susceptible to confounding block boundary artifacts with authentic tampering edges when compression quality is low.

\subsection{Attention Mechanisms in Deep Networks}
Attention mechanisms enable convolutional networks to emphasize salient feature locations while suppressing irrelevant background signals. Hu et al. \cite{hu2018squeeze} introduced Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) networks, computing channel-wise modulation weights via global average pooling. Woo et al. \cite{woo2018cbam} proposed the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM), combining channel and spatial attention sequentially.

While effective in general vision tasks, standard attention formulations present distinct drawbacks in compressed forensic scenarios. SE pooling removes spatial coordinate information completely \cite{hou2021coordinate}. Standard two dimensional spatial attention captures broad spatial regions without directional selectivity, making it susceptible to locking onto repetitive two dimensional JPEG grid edges. Hou et al. \cite{hou2021coordinate} overcame these challenges with Coordinate Attention, which factorizes spatial pooling into orthogonal horizontal and vertical one dimensional feature vectors. This design captures position sensitive forensic signatures with negligible computational overhead, providing directional resilience against compression artifacts.

\section{Proposed Methodology}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering

\label{fig:arch}
\end{figure}

The overall pipeline of the proposed \textbf{Coord-EfficientNet-B3} architecture is illustrated in Fig. \ref{fig:arch}. The framework comprises three core stages: (1) deep feature extraction via an EfficientNet-B3 backbone \cite{tan2019efficientnet}, (2) directional feature refinement through Coordinate Attention \cite{hou2021coordinate}, and (3) a multi-stage classification head producing the posterior probability.

\subsection{Backbone Feature Extraction}
Given an input face crop $X \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 224 \times 224}$, the image is processed through an EfficientNet-B3 backbone pretrained on ImageNet \cite{tan2019efficientnet}. The network applies mobile inverted bottleneck convolutions (MBConv) across seven stages, yielding an intermediate feature representation:
\begin{equation}
F = \Phi_{\text{backbone}}(X) \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H \times W}
\end{equation}
where $C = 1536$ represents the feature channel depth and $H = W = 7$ denotes the spatial resolution.

\subsection{Coordinate Attention Formulation}
To preserve fine tampering traces and mitigate isotropic compression noise, Coordinate Attention is embedded immediately following the final convolutional stage. Standard global average pooling collapses spatial dimensions into a scalar, discarding spatial context. Coordinate Attention instead factorizes spatial aggregation into two one dimensional feature encodings.

For channel $c$, feature values are pooled along horizontal and vertical spatial dimensions separately. Horizontal pooling across width $W$ produces vector $z_c^h \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times 1}$, while vertical pooling across height $H$ produces vector $z_c^w \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times W}$:
\begin{equation}
z_c^h(h) = \frac{1}{W} \sum_{j=0}^{W-1} F_c(h, j), \quad z_c^w(w) = \frac{1}{H} \sum_{i=0}^{H-1} F_c(i, w)
\end{equation}

The pooled representations $z^h$ and $z^w$ are concatenated along the spatial dimension and passed through a shared $1 \times 1$ convolutional transformation with a channel reduction ratio $r = 32$, reducing channel dimensionality to $C_r = \max(8, \lfloor C/r \rfloor) = 48$. After Batch Normalization and a Hard-Swish activation, the intermediate feature map is split back along spatial axes into horizontal and vertical components. Two distinct $1 \times 1$ convolutions restore channel dimensionality to $C = 1536$, and sigmoid functions yield direction-aware coordinate attention weights $g^h \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H \times 1}$ and $g^w \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times 1 \times W}$. The modulated feature representation $Y \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H \times W}$ is computed via element-wise multiplication:
\begin{equation}
Y_c(i, j) = F_c(i, j) \times g_c^h(i) \times g_c^w(j)
\end{equation}
This operation enables the network to locate forgery artifacts along one coordinate axis while retaining a broad receptive field along the perpendicular axis.

\subsection{Classification Head and Decision Formulation}
The refined feature tensor $Y$ undergoes Global Average Pooling, producing a 1536-dimensional feature descriptor $\bar{y}$. This descriptor is passed into a three-stage fully connected classification head:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Dense layer: $1536 \to 128$, with ReLU activation and Dropout ($p = 0.30$)
    \item Dense layer: $128 \to 64$, with ReLU activation and Dropout ($p = 0.20$)
    \item Output layer: $64 \to 1$, producing unnormalized logit $z$.
\end{itemize}
The estimated probability of an image being synthetic is computed via the sigmoid activation:
\begin{equation}
P(\text{Fake} \mid X) = \sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}
\end{equation}
A binary decision is assigned using threshold $\tau$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } P(\text{Fake} \mid X) \ge \tau \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stochastic Compression-Aware Training}
During training, input images are augmented with stochastic JPEG compression with probability $p_{\text{jpeg}} = 0.50$. When triggered, quality factors are sampled uniformly:
\begin{equation}
\text{QF} \sim \text{Uniform}(\{20, 40, 50, 60, 80\})
\end{equation}
Optimization minimizes the Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) objective:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = - [y \log \sigma(z) + (1 - y) \log (1 - \sigma(z))]
\end{equation}
Training uses the Adam optimizer with learning rate $\eta = 10^{-4}$ and mini-batch size $B = 16$ across 10 epochs. A two-stage transfer learning schedule is employed: the first two epochs freeze the backbone to stabilize the classifier head, followed by eight epochs of end-to-end fine-tuning.

\subsection{Benchmark Dataset and Partitioning Protocol}
Experimental investigations were conducted on the 140,000 facial image benchmark repository. The authentic class comprises human facial portraits from Flickr-Faces-HQ (FFHQ), while the synthetic class consists of high-resolution artificial faces synthesized by NVIDIA StyleGAN.

To ensure experimental integrity and prevent data leakage, a strict partition was constructed:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Training Set:} 7,200 images (3,600 Real, 3,600 Fake)
    \item \textbf{Validation Set:} 800 clean images (400 Real, 400 Fake) used for checkpoint selection
    \item \textbf{Test Set:} 2,000 images (1,000 Real, 1,000 Fake) evaluated across multiple compression levels
    \item \textbf{Unseen Holdout Set:} 200 distinct facial images (100 Real, 100 Fake) drawn from the untouched benchmark test directory to assess out-of-distribution generalization.
\end{itemize}
SHA-256 and difference-hashing (dHash) audits verified that no duplicate images crossed between splits.

\subsection{Multi-Condition Compression Regimes}
To replicate transmission conditions across social media networks, the test images were evaluated across six distinct regimes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clean:} Original, uncompressed images
    \item \textbf{QF80:} High quality compression (minimal forensic loss)
    \item \textbf{QF60:} Moderate compression (standard messaging apps)
    \item \textbf{QF50:} Medium compression (balanced web transmission)
    \item \textbf{QF40:} Substantial compression (bandwidth-constrained channels)
    \item \textbf{QF20:} Severe compression (aggressive social media re-encoding)
\end{itemize}
Evaluating 2,000 test images across six conditions produces 12,000 total inference runs per model architecture. Testing the 200 holdout images across all six conditions yields an additional 1,200 inference evaluations.

\subsection{Implementation Details}
All models were implemented in PyTorch using the timm library. Training was executed on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3050 Ti Laptop GPU. Images were resized to $224 \times 224$ and normalized using ImageNet channel statistics. Evaluation was conducted in strict inference mode with deterministic seeds.

\section{Results and Discussions}

\subsection{Multi-Regime Compression Performance}
Table \ref{tab:master} presents the quantitative results across all six compression tiers evaluated at the standard threshold $\tau = 0.50$. The proposed Coord-EfficientNet-B3 model achieves superior classification accuracy across every quality level.

On pristine, uncompressed imagery, the proposed architecture improves accuracy from 97.40\% to 97.80\%, reducing misclassifications from 52 to 44 (-15.4\%). When lossy compression is introduced, the accuracy advantage of the proposed model becomes more pronounced. As depicted in Fig. \ref{fig:acc_curve}, while both models experience accuracy degradation under lower quality factors, the performance drop for the baseline model is substantially steeper. At QF20, the baseline accuracy drops to 94.80\% with 104 total errors, whereas Coord-EfficientNet-B3 preserves 95.85\% accuracy with 83 errors, representing an error reduction of 20.2\%.

Aggregated across the 10,000 compressed test evaluations (QF80 through QF20), the proposed framework delivers notable improvements:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Overall misclassifications drop from 354 to 303, achieving an aggregate error reduction of 14.4\%.
    \item False positive errors decrease from 128 to 97, achieving a 24.2\% reduction in false alarms.
    \item False negative errors decline from 226 to 206, representing an 8.8\% reduction in missed synthetic faces.
    \item Matthews Correlation Coefficient increases from 0.9295 to 0.9397, reflecting greater predictive stability across classes.
\end{itemize}

Fig. \ref{fig:error_breakdown} illustrates the distribution of false positives and false negatives across each quality factor. Under mild to moderate compression (QF80, QF60, QF50), the baseline produces noticeable spikes in false positives (reaching 36 at QF50), caused by convolutional filters registering high-frequency DCT boundary transitions as synthetic edges. In contrast, Coordinate Attention limits false positives to 25 at QF50 and 10 at QF80.

To establish statistical significance, a paired McNemar test was conducted over the 10,000 paired compressed predictions. The contingency table indicates 9,540 concordant correct classifications, 246 concordant incorrect classifications, 157 instances where Coord-EfficientNet-B3 was correct while Baseline failed, and 57 instances where Baseline was correct while Coord-EfficientNet-B3 failed. The resulting test statistic is $\chi^2 = 10.55$, corresponding to a two-tailed $p$-value of $p = 0.00116$. Because $p < 0.01$, the performance superiority of the proposed framework over the standard EfficientNet-B3 baseline is statistically significant.

\subsection{Probability Distribution and Confidence Drift Analysis}
Analyzing the output probability distributions reveals the underlying failure modes under heavy compression. On pristine images, both models produce sharp, bimodal probability distributions: authentic images yield probabilities tightly clustered around $P(\text{Fake}) \in [0.00, 0.05]$, while synthetic images cluster near $P(\text{Fake}) \in [0.95, 1.00]$.

However, under severe compression (QF20), the baseline EfficientNet-B3 exhibits substantial representation drift. The mean predicted posterior probability for synthetic images drops from 0.982 on clean images to 0.741 at QF20, with a heavy distribution tail crossing below the 0.50 classification boundary. This probability collapse causes 66 false negatives. By contrast, Coord-EfficientNet-B3 retains significantly higher predictive confidence: the mean synthetic probability remains at 0.864 at QF20, maintaining distinct separation between genuine and manipulated classes. The directional 1D pooling projections prevent gradient attenuation, ensuring reliable class boundary separation even when 2D spatial details are corrupted by compression noise.

\subsection{Limitations}
While the proposed framework shows substantial robustness improvements, two limitations should be noted:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The current setup evaluates static $256 \times 256$ face crops. Real world video manipulations involve temporal inconsistencies across consecutive frames that static spatial models cannot exploit.
    \item Experiments utilized simulated JPEG compression with standard quantization matrices. In practical environments, social media platforms apply proprietary multi-stage compression pipelines that combine spatial downsampling with video codecs (e.g., H.264, AV1).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
This paper introduced Coord-EfficientNet-B3, an attention enhanced deep learning architecture designed for robust deepfake detection under lossy social media compression. By decomposing spatial attention into orthogonal one dimensional horizontal and vertical coordinate representations, the proposed model captures localized manipulation boundaries while filtering out spurious Discrete Cosine Transform block ringing. Across 12,000 multi-quality factor evaluations, the framework reduced overall compressed classification errors by 14.4\% and suppressed false positives by 24.2\% ($p = 0.00116$, McNemar test) compared to the unaugmented baseline. On an unseen holdout benchmark of 200 facial portraits, the architecture halved missed synthetic faces (-50.0\% false negatives) and reduced total errors by 49.1\%. Furthermore, probability distribution analysis confirmed that coordinate attention prevents confidence drift under severe compression. Future investigations will explore integrating spatio-temporal coordinate attention into video forensic backbones and evaluating performance against emerging diffusion-based generative models.
